# Mayo
[bgm acoustic.11]
WALTER: You've heard this story from young Ellie, and you've heard it from Agnes Pike, who misses nothing, God help us all. Now you'll hear it from me. And I'll tell you the truth, because I'm too old to remember lies.
WALTER: I was born in County Mayo, in the west of Ireland, in nineteen fifty-three. We had a small farm. Twelve cows, a few sheep, and a lot of rocks. There were nine of us children, and there wasn't enough land for all of us, so when I was fifteen, my mother packed my clothes into a cardboard suitcase, gave me a kiss and a loaf of bread, and put me on the boat to England.
WALTER: Thousands of young Irish lads [[emigrated|emigrate]] in those days. Most of them went to London to build roads. I [[ended up|end up]] in Yorkshire, by accident, because a man on the train told me there was work on a farm near Thornby.
WALTER: The farmer was Jack Hartley. Ellie's grandad. He was a hard man and a fair one, and he taught me everything. How to milk by hand. How to [[look after|look after]] a sick calf. How to read the sky in the morning.
WALTER: Ellie tells people I've worked on that farm for fifty years. It's fifty-eight. But who's counting.
WALTER: I never married. I nearly did, once. She married the postman. I don't blame her. He had a car.
WALTER: So the farm became my family. Jack, and then his son Tom, and then little Ellie. And the cows. Always the cows.
WALTER: People say cows are stupid. Those people have never spent time with cows. A cow knows who's kind to her. She remembers. She has friends in the [[herd]], and enemies too. And if you raise one from a calf, the way I raised Duchess, she'll follow you to the end of the earth.
[bgm off]

# The Window
[bgm mystery.14]
WALTER: On Tuesday evening I was in the yard when that man in the suit arrived. Delaney. Shiny shoes. Teeth like a toilet advert.
WALTER: I saw Ellie listening at the kitchen window, and I'll admit it, I listened too. I was standing behind her, so I heard more than she did, because my ears are older but my head was nearer the gap.
WALTER: I heard him say the price. That was bad enough. But then I heard him say something else.
DELANEY (quiet): She'd go to our centre in Cheshire. It's all indoors. Very efficient. She'd never need to see a field again.
WALTER: Never need to see a field again.
WALTER: Do you know what a cow is like in spring, when you let her out on the grass for the first time after a long winter inside? She runs. A big heavy cow, six hundred kilos, running and jumping like a lamb. Kicking her back legs in the air. Every farmer has seen it. It makes grown men laugh out loud.
WALTER: And this fella wanted to put her in a shed for the rest of her life, and call it efficient.
WALTER: I'm not against progress. I've seen [[intensive farming|intensive farming]] up close, and I know why some farms do it. The world wants cheap milk. Somebody has to pay. But not her. Not Duchess.
WALTER: I walked home. I didn't eat my tea. I sat by the fire, and I listened to the storm, and I thought about it for three hours.
WALTER: At half past one, the rain stopped.
WALTER: And I stood up, and I put on my coat.
[bgm off]

# The Night
[amb night]
[bgm spooky.15 0.12]
WALTER: I took my lantern, because I don't trust those little torches. I took my stick, because I can't walk far without it. And I took a packet of ginger biscuits, because I always do.
WALTER: Bess came out of her kennel when she heard me. She didn't bark. Why would she? She's known me since the day she was born. I gave her a scratch behind the ears and told her to go back to bed, and she did.
WALTER: I slid back the bolt on the back door of the barn, and I went in, and I said, very quietly, "Duchess."
WALTER: She got up straight away. She came to me in the dark like she'd been waiting.
WALTER: Now, I'm old, but I'm not daft. I knew about the collar. Ellie had shown me that app a hundred times. "Look, Walter, this is how much she chewed today. Look, Walter, this is where she is on the map." So I knew that if I walked off with Duchess, the little blue dot would walk off with us.
WALTER: But I also knew that down at the bottom of the valley, by the beck, there's no phone signal at all. Ellie complains about it every single day. So the collar would go quiet down there. And that would give me a bit of time.
WALTER: I led her out, and I bolted the door behind us. Habit. Fifty-eight years of closing gates. And we walked down the field to the water.

WALTER: Walking a cow along a stream in the middle of the night, with a bad hip and a lantern, is not something I'd recommend. The water was cold and fast after the storm. Twice I nearly fell. Duchess waited for me both times. She's more [[patient]] than I am.
WALTER: And then I heard a splashing behind us. And there, in the moonlight, with water up to his knees and a face like the devil himself, was Napoleon.
WALTER: That goat had escaped again. Of course he had. And he'd smelled my biscuits from half a mile away.
WALTER: I said, "Go home, you terrible animal." He did not go home. He followed us all the way along the beck, making a noise like a rusty door every time I didn't give him a biscuit.
WALTER: And that's when I had my idea.
[amb off]
WALTER: Low Gill is an old stone barn at the bottom of the fell. Nobody uses it any more. It's [[derelict]]. Half the roof is gone. But one end is still dry, and I'd been keeping a few bales of hay in there for years, for emergencies. Don't ask me what emergencies. I'm Irish. We prepare for things.
WALTER: I got Duchess inside. Then I took the collar off her. It was hard work, with cold fingers. And I put it on Napoleon.
WALTER: It was far too big for him. It hung round his neck like a mayor's chain.
WALTER: Then I walked out of the barn and up the path towards the fell, and every ten steps, I dropped a ginger biscuit. All the way up the hill. Half a packet.
WALTER: And Napoleon followed the biscuits, the way a [[politician]] follows money. Up and up and up, towards the old quarry. On Victor Crane's land.
WALTER: The minute he got up there, out of the valley, the collar found a signal again. Twenty-five past two. And the little blue dot appeared on Ellie's phone, climbing up the fell like a mountain goat. Because it was one.
WALTER: I'm not proud of it. Well. I'm a little bit proud of it.

# Two Days
[bgm piano.16]
WALTER: For two days, I lived a double life. Like a spy. A spy with a bad hip.
WALTER: In the daytime, I was on the fell with everybody else, shouting "Duchess!", pretending to search. Every time I looked at Ellie's face, I felt sick. I'd never seen that girl cry before. Not even when her grandad died. She was too busy looking after everybody else.
WALTER: And in the dark, at five in the morning and again at five in the evening, I walked down to Low Gill to milk her.
WALTER: People forget about that part. A dairy cow can't just stand in a barn and wait. She makes thirty litres of milk a day. If she isn't milked, it hurts her. So twice a day, I sat on an old bucket, the way Jack Hartley taught me sixty years ago, and I milked her by hand.
WALTER: Then I poured the milk away into the beck. That broke my heart a little bit. Thirty litres of the best milk in Yorkshire, going down the stream for the fish.
WALTER: On Thursday, Ellie showed me her phone. Her picture of Duchess had [[gone viral|go viral]]. Fifty thousand people. A hundred thousand. People in Japan, in Canada, in Brazil, all looking for a cow that was eating hay in a barn two fields away from them.
WALTER: That night I sat in my kitchen, and I asked myself the question I should have asked on Tuesday. What exactly was I going to do?
WALTER: Hide her at Low Gill for the next ten years? Milk her by hand till I'm ninety? Go to prison at seventy-three, for [[theft]]?
WALTER: I didn't have a plan. I'd only had a feeling. And a feeling isn't a plan. It's just a feeling with wet boots.
WALTER: Then, on Friday afternoon, Agnes Pike knocked on my door, and drank my tea, and looked at my boots, and looked at my stick, and looked at my biscuit tin. And she didn't say a word about any of it.
WALTER: That's when I knew I was in real trouble.
[bgm off]

# Saturday, Six A.M.
[sfx knock]
WALTER: She came back at six o'clock on Saturday morning. Show day. She was holding a flask.
AGNES: Morning, Walter. I've brought my own tea, so you needn't bother.
WALTER: You'd better come in, so.
[bgm acoustic.17]
WALTER: She sat in my chair by the fire, and she poured two cups, and she gave me one. And then she told me exactly what I'd done. Every bit of it. The bolt. The beck. The boots. The goat. She even knew about the biscuits on the path. She'd gone up there on Friday evening and found three of them that Napoleon had missed.
AGNES: There was only one thing I couldn't [[work out|work out]] at first. Why would a thief walk a cow down a freezing stream in the middle of the night, at seventy-three, with a hip like yours?
WALTER: And?
AGNES: And then I realised. You weren't stealing her. You were saving her. You heard what that man said about the shed.
WALTER: I didn't answer for a long time. Then I said, "Are you going to [[arrest]] me, Agnes?"
AGNES (warm): I'm retired, Walter. I couldn't arrest a bus. But I'll tell you what I think. You can't keep a dairy cow hidden in a barn forever. And you can't keep a secret like this in a village like Thornby. The truth is like a cow, Walter. Sooner or later, it always comes home.
WALTER: So what do I do?
AGNES: The show starts at ten. Duchess is entered in the cattle class at eleven. You've got four hours to wash a cow.

# The Thornby Show
[music sports.18 10]
[bgm sports.19 0.12]
WALTER: If you've never been to a country show in England, I'll try to [[describe]] it. Picture a big green field full of white tents. A brass band playing. Sheepdogs. Tractors, old and new. Prize vegetables. A tent full of cakes, where very serious women judge other women's sponge cakes, and nobody ever forgives anybody. Ice cream. Mud. Thousands of people in wellies.
WALTER: And at the centre of it all, the main ring, where the animals are judged.
WALTER: I walked Duchess from Low Gill to the showground. Two miles, along the road. She was washed and brushed and shining like a new penny. Agnes walked beside us with her flask. Napoleon followed behind, wearing nothing at all, thank God.
WALTER: And the funny thing was, people recognised her. On the road. A car slowed down, and a woman put her head out of the window and screamed, "It's her! It's the cow from the internet!"
WALTER: By the time we reached the gate of the showground, there were about fifty people walking behind us, taking photos. It was like a royal wedding. With a goat.
JUDGE: Ladies and gentlemen, we have a late entry in the Dairy Shorthorn class... Well, I never. Ladies and gentlemen, I believe that's Duchess!
[sfx applause]
WALTER: The noise. I've never heard anything like it. People cheering for a cow.
WALTER: And then I saw them. Tom and Ellie, standing by the ring. They'd only come because Ellie didn't want Victor Crane to win without someone watching.
WALTER: Ellie ran. Straight under the rope, across the ring, and threw her arms round Duchess's neck. Duchess licked her hair. Ellie was laughing and crying at the same time. I've never seen anybody so [[relieved]].
ELLIE (excited): Walter! You found her! Where was she? Where did you find her?
WALTER: And I looked at Tom. And Tom looked at me. Tom Hartley has known me since he was born. I taught him to ride a bike. I taught him to drive a tractor. He knew me better than anybody.
WALTER: I watched his face change. He understood.
