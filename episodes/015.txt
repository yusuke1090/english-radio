# How It Works
[bgm acoustic.147 0.1]
GUY: Okay, Molly. Explain this to me like I'm five years old. What happens in a sheepdog trial?
MOLLY: Right. You've got one handler, that's the shepherd. One dog. And five sheep. The sheep start right at the far end of the field. About four hundred metres away, up that hill. Can you see them? Those little white dots?
GUY: I see them. They look like popcorn.
MOLLY: They do look like popcorn. Now, the handler stands at a post down here, and sends the dog out to get the sheep. And then, using only a whistle and their voice, the handler has to guide the dog, so the dog moves the sheep around a course. Through gates. Around a post. Into a little circle. And finally into a small pen. And they've got fifteen minutes.
GUY: And the handler can't move?
MOLLY: Not until the end. They stand at the post the whole time. The dog does all the running. The handler does all the thinking. Well. Most of the thinking. The good dogs do quite a lot of thinking too.
GUY: How does the dog know where to go?
MOLLY: Whistles, mostly. Every handler has their own set. One whistle means "go left". One means "go right". One means "stop". One means "walk forward". The dog learns them like words. Some dogs know twenty different whistles.
GUY: Twenty! I've got a dog at home who doesn't know his own name. He thinks his name is "Get Off The Couch".
MOLLY: And the scoring is simple. Every run starts with a hundred points. And the judges take points off for every little mistake. If the sheep go off the line, if the dog is too rough, if the sheep miss a gate. [[The highest]] score wins.
GUY: So it's like gymnastics. With sheep.
MOLLY: It's exactly like gymnastics with sheep. And the sheep didn't sign up for it.
