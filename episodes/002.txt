# The Copper Kettle
[bgm piano.2]
AGNES: My name is Agnes Pike. For thirty years I was a detective with West Yorkshire Police. I've chased burglars across rooftops in Bradford. I've sat in cold cars all night outside houses in Leeds, eating cold chips and waiting for people who never came out.
AGNES: Four years ago, I retired. I bought a little tea room in Thornby, I got three hives of bees, and I promised myself I would never [[investigate]] anything ever again.
AGNES: For four years, the most serious crime in Thornby was somebody stealing the flowers from the churchyard. It [[turned out|turn out]] to be a deer.
AGNES: I'll be honest with you. I was bored out of my mind.
AGNES: So when Ellie Hartley rang my doorbell on Wednesday night and said, "Somebody stole our cow and they put her collar on a goat," I tried very hard to look serious. But inside, I was [[delighted]].
AGNES: I sat her down in the kitchen, behind the tea room, and I made her a hot chocolate. Then I asked her to tell me everything. From the beginning. Slowly.
AGNES: She took out a little notebook. That's when I started to like her.
ELLIE: One. The barn door was bolted from the outside. Two. There were no tyre marks. Three. Bess didn't bark. Four. Somebody put Duchess's collar on a goat. Five...
AGNES: She stopped.
AGNES: And five?
ELLIE (sad): Five. A man from a company called Northstar Genetics came to see my dad on Tuesday. I think Dad's going to sell her. He won't tell me.
AGNES: She looked at her hot chocolate for a long time. I didn't say anything. Sometimes the best thing a detective can do is keep quiet.
AGNES: Then I said, "Right. I'll help you. On two conditions. First, you do exactly what I say. Second, no more posting things on the internet."
ELLIE: Why not?
AGNES: Because, Ellie, whoever took that cow is reading it too.

# The Barn
[bgm mystery.10]
[amb birds]
AGNES: On Thursday morning I was at Hartley Farm at seven o'clock, in my wellies, with my old notebook. Tom Hartley didn't look pleased to see me. Farmers don't like people who ask questions. Neither do criminals. It's one of the things they have in common.
TOM: With respect, Mrs Pike, the police have already been.
AGNES: I'm sure they have. I'm only here for a look. Pretend I'm a tourist.
AGNES: I started with the barn door. The one at the back, leading out to the lane. A heavy wooden door, with a big iron bolt on the outside.
AGNES: Now, [[bear in mind]] what a thief is like. A thief is in a hurry. A thief is nervous. When a thief takes something, he leaves the door open behind him and runs.
AGNES: But this thief had closed the door. And bolted it. Neatly. So that the other seventy-nine cows wouldn't wander out into the lane.
AGNES: In my experience, thieves don't close gates. Farmers do. It's an [[instinct]]. You grow up on a farm, you close every gate behind you, even in your sleep.
AGNES: So. Our thief knew about cows. And cared about them. I wrote that down.
AGNES: Then I looked at the ground.
AGNES: On Tuesday night there had been [[heavy rain]]. I'd checked the [[rainfall]] figures from the little weather station at the school. It rained hard from about ten o'clock, and stopped just after half past one.
AGNES: Duchess left the barn at eight minutes to two. So any footprints made after that would still be there, in the soft mud, as clear as writing in a book.
AGNES: And they were.
AGNES: Here, Ellie. Come and look. Tell me what you see.
ELLIE: Cow prints. Hooves. Going out of the back door.
AGNES: Which way?
ELLIE: Not up the lane. Down. Down the field towards the beck.
AGNES: Exactly. The collar said she ended up on the fell. But her feet say she went down to the water first. Feet are more honest than collars. What else?
ELLIE: Boot prints. Next to her. Somebody was walking beside her.
AGNES: How big?
ELLIE: Not very big. Smaller than my dad's.
AGNES: Tom Hartley wears a size eleven boot. These were a size eight, at most. And look closer. The left boot pushes deeper into the mud than the right. Every single time.
ELLIE: So he was heavier on one side?
AGNES: Or limping. And here, next to the right boot. Every second step. A little round hole in the mud.
ELLIE (excited): A stick!
AGNES: A stick. Or a shepherd's crook. Don't jump to conclusions, Ellie. Jumping is for goats.
AGNES: We followed the prints down the field to the beck, the little stream at the bottom of the valley. And there, at the edge of the water, they stopped.
ELLIE: Where did they go?
AGNES: Into the water. Whoever it was walked the cow along the stream, so there'd be no prints to follow. It's an old trick. Cattle thieves were doing it two hundred years ago.

AGNES: And then, while we were standing there, Bess came running down the field, wagging her tail. She jumped up at Ellie, and then she jumped up at me, and licked my glasses.
AGNES: Does she do that with everyone?
ELLIE: She's friendly when she knows you. But if it's a stranger, she barks like mad.
AGNES: And on Tuesday night, she didn't bark at all.

# High Crane
[bgm comedy.2]
AGNES: Now, if you're investigating something, you have to start with the obvious suspects... Actually, I'll tell you a secret. The obvious suspect is almost never the answer. But you have to talk to them anyway, or people complain.

AGNES: Victor owns High Crane Farm, which is the biggest farm in the valley, and he wants everybody to know it. He has a new tractor every year. He has a gold watch. He has a portrait of himself in his own hall. It's an oil painting. He's holding a trophy in it.
AGNES: He met me at the door, holding his long shepherd's crook. He takes it everywhere. I think he sleeps with it.

# Friday Night

AGNES: And one more thing. The thing that nobody else had thought of. Duchess is a dairy cow. A dairy cow has to be milked. Twice a day, every day. If she isn't, she's in pain.
