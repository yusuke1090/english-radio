# Sarcasm School
SONIA: And finally, it's time for something educational. Welcome to Sarcasm School.
RYAN: Because if you're learning British English, it's not enough to understand the words. You have to understand what people really mean.
SONIA: And British people very often don't say what they mean. We use understatement, which means making something sound much smaller than it is.
RYAN: So let's look at some examples. Sonia, the phrase, that's not ideal.
SONIA: When a British person says, that's not ideal, it can mean anything from a small problem to a complete disaster. If your car is on fire, a British person might look at it and say, well, that's not ideal.
RYAN: Next one. Quite good.
SONIA: Ah, this one is dangerous. In American English, quite good often means very good. In British English, it usually means it was OK, but a bit disappointing.
RYAN: So if your British boss says your report is quite good…
SONIA: You should probably rewrite it.
RYAN: And the opposite: not bad. If a British person says, not bad, about your cooking, they probably loved it.
SONIA: It's very confusing. Quite good is bad, and not bad is good.
RYAN: Welcome to Britain. Next one. With the greatest respect.
SONIA: This one means: I am about to tell you that you are completely wrong. And maybe an idiot.
RYAN: With the greatest respect, Sonia, your driving is terrible.
SONIA: See? No respect at all. And then there's: I'll bear it in mind.
RYAN: Which means: I have already forgotten what you said, and I will never think about it again.
SONIA: And: that's an interesting idea.
RYAN: Which means: that's a terrible idea, and please stop talking.
SONIA: Let's practise. Ryan, I have an idea for the show. What if we did a whole episode about the history of the hedge?
RYAN: That's an interesting idea. I'll [[bear it in mind|bear in mind]].
SONIA: You see, listeners? He hates it.
RYAN: With the greatest respect, it's the worst idea I've ever heard.
SONIA: And sarcasm also means saying the opposite of what you mean, in a very flat voice. For example, Ryan, how was your Saturday?
RYAN: Oh, brilliant. Best day of my life. I stood in the rain for twenty minutes, and then I went on a lovely bus trip to a car park.
SONIA: Lovely. Sounds great.
RYAN: Thanks. I'm [[over the moon]] about it.
SONIA: So, a little tip for our listeners. If a British person says something positive, but their face doesn't move at all, they probably mean the opposite.
RYAN: And if you're not sure, just say, ha, and nod. It works most of the time.
SONIA: That's actually the most useful advice we've ever given on this show.
RYAN: That's quite good, isn't it?
SONIA: Quite good? Oh no. Now I have to rewrite the whole show.
RYAN: Ha. Well, that's the end of our Late Night Special. If you enjoyed it, let us know. If you didn't…
SONIA: We'll bear it in mind.
